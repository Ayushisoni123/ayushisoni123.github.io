Cases 1 & 2: Full-Journey Priority + Exact Short-Leg Auto-Swap

Full-journey buyers (MAS → NDLS) receive early priority. Closer to departure (T-6h to T-1h), a partial-leg buyer (MAS → NGP) receives confirmed travel while sitting in a berth-assignment buffer; if an exact MAS → NGP ticket is flagged later, CRIS auto-swaps them (honoring Lower Berth locks) and frees the long-haul seat intact.

Cases 3 & 4: Complementary Pairing & T-30m Chart Settlement

When a complementary NGP → NDLS buyer books the remaining leg, both passengers share one physical berth across two station-indexed PNRs. If the second leg remains unsold at T-30m, the flagger receives the MAS → NGP fare at Second Chart Freeze — preserving ₹0 cash loss to Indian Railways.

Why True Station-Indexed Split-PNRs Are Mandatory
  • Prevents False No-Show (NTU) Cancellation: Leaving a second-leg buyer (NGP → NDLS) on an unsplit MAS → NDLS PNR would cause the Chennai TTE to mark the berth empty at origin and reallocate it to RAC before Nagpur. True station-indexed PNR splitting displays Leg 1 (MAS→NGP) then Leg 2 (NGP→NDLS) on the TTE HHT.
  • Why Ship v1 Single-Leg First: Validating adoption, Aadhaar-OTP caps, and T-1h HHT sync on the Southern Railway (SR) Express pilot first eliminates mid-journey handover friction during MVP validation.
